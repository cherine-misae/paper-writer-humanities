# Evidence and Quotation Protocol

## Evidence ledger

Maintain `evidence_ledger.md` as the bridge between sources and prose:

```markdown
| Claim ID | Consequential claim | Evidence type | Source key | Locator | Access level | Use | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | ... | primary text / scholarly argument / contextual fact | ... | page, chapter, section, line, folio, paragraph, or stable fragment | full-text | support / complicate / oppose | verified / limited / unresolved |
```

Create entries for thesis-bearing claims, characterization of scholarship, direct quotations, historical context, and claims of influence or reception.

## Direct quotation

Quote only text inspected in this run or supplied by the user. Preserve exact wording and meaningful typography. Record source language, edition or translation, translator, stable locator, and any omission, interpolation, or emphasis. Do not reconstruct quotations from memory, quote search snippets, silently modernize spelling, or silently translate. Label the report's own translations.

## Paraphrase and attribution

Keep paraphrases within the inspected source's scope. Distinguish `source says`, `scholar argues`, and `this report argues`. Do not attribute an entire book's thesis from an abstract, review, or catalog description. Qualify statements based on partial access.

## Close reading

A close-reading paragraph normally contains a focused textual feature, precise observation, explanation of its bearing on the claim, engagement with scholarship or an alternative reading, and connection to the larger argument. If the passage cannot be inspected, present the point as a research lead rather than a finding.

Dates, publication history, reception, influence, intention, and causal statements require explicit evidence. Similarity does not establish influence; sequence does not establish causation. Preserve scholarly disagreement and note whether competing positions rely on different editions, definitions, or evidence.
