# Core 03 — Prose and Attribution

## Three voices, always distinct

Every paragraph should make clear which of these is speaking:

- **the evidence says** — description of a text, source, participant, document, or result;
- **a scholar argues** — a named position in the literature;
- **this report argues** — Carrel's own analysis, offered for the researcher's judgment.

Never blur these. Profile §7 names the evidence voice for its method (e.g. "the passage reads", "the participant said", "the estimate shows").

## Prose rules

- One argumentative task per paragraph.
- Qualify claims to the strength of the evidence; do not claim novelty until `source_coverage.md` supports it.
- Do not force first-person plural.
- Analyze quotations and evidence; never let them stand as proof.
- No literature dumps, ornamental theory citations, market statistics, or generic urgency claims.
- Preserve scholarly disagreement and note when positions rest on different editions, definitions, data, or evidence.
- Do not infer intention, identity, influence, or causation without ledger evidence.

## Sections common to all profiles

The profile (§6) sets the full section list and order. These three appear in every profile:

- **Abstract or executive summary** — problem, evidence base, approach, central argument, significance. No citations unless the genre requires them.
- **Introduction** — question and stakes, evidence and scope, intervention in the literature, appropriately qualified thesis or research question, path of the argument.
- **Conclusion** — what the analysis changes about the problem, implications, further research grounded in unresolved evidence.

Use conceptual section titles rather than "Analysis 1". Merge, rename, or reorder sections when the material calls for it, and record the reason in `argument_map.md`.
