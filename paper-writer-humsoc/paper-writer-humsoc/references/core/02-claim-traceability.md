# Core 02 — Claim Traceability and Quotation

## Evidence ledger

`evidence_ledger.md` bridges evidence and prose. Core columns (never removed; the profile §5 appends more):

```markdown
| Claim ID | Consequential claim | Evidence type | Source key | Locator | Access level | Use | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | ... | see profile §2 | ... | page / section / fragment / ID — see profile | full-text | support / complicate / oppose | verified / limited / unresolved |
```

Create rows for thesis-bearing claims, characterizations of scholarship, every direct quotation, contextual and historical facts, and claims of influence, reception, causation, or generalization.

## Quotation — method-independent rules

Quote only material inspected in this run or supplied by the user. Preserve exact wording and meaningful typography. Record any omission, interpolation, emphasis, or editorial change. Do not reconstruct wording from memory, quote search snippets, or quote from an item whose access level is `abstract-only` or `metadata-only`. The profile adds rules for its own evidence (editions and translations, transcripts, data tables).

## Paraphrase and attribution

Keep paraphrases within the inspected scope of the source. Do not attribute a whole work's thesis from an abstract, review, news report, or catalog record. When a scholar's position is characterized from such a source, the qualification appears in the sentence itself (e.g. "as reported in X", "according to the abstract"), not only in a limitations section, and the ledger row's access level matches. If a position cannot be characterized from inspected material, name the work as relevant and stop there.

## Claims that need explicit evidence

Dates, publication history, reception, influence, intention, causation, and generalizations across cases or people require explicit evidence in the ledger. Similarity does not establish influence; sequence does not establish causation; a few instances do not establish a pattern. If evidence cannot be inspected, present the point as a lead for further research, not a finding.
